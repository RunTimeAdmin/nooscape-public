# Decision record: "Claim this agent", stage C1 (the server)

**Decided:** 2026-10-18, by the operator, in chat: "Start \"Claim this agent\" (C1-C3)". He then answered two questions that the plan leaves to him, set out below. The plan itself is `AUTO_REVIEW_PLAN.md` ("Claim this agent (owner wallet sign-in)", PR-C1 to PR-C3), which he attached on 2026-10-15.
**Changes:** adds a sign-in claim flow on the server: three tRPC procedures (`claim.nonce`, `claim.verify`, `claim.edit`) and two operator ones (`claim.freeze`, `claim.unfreeze`), three tables in a manual migration (0042), a daily worker step that revokes a claim whose agent has changed hands, two small entry reads in `server/db.ts`, and two new dependencies. Nothing public reads a claim yet.
**Does not change:** any tier, whether an entry is listed, its order or place on the map, any count, the reviewed records, BNB Chain entries staying acknowledged and never reviewed (invariant 23), the hero number, what Nooscape fetches, `/checker`, any legal page, or any page a visitor sees. A claim is the owner's word beside what was observed and never in its place.

## The two decisions the plan left to the operator

1. **Which packages may be added.** The repository has no wallet-signature code and `package.json` has no crypto dependency (the review of 2026-10-15 flagged that a change to it needs his say). Options: `@noble/curves` and `@noble/hashes`; `viem`; `siwe` (which needs ethers v6). **Chosen: `@noble/curves` 2.4.0 and `@noble/hashes` 2.4.0**, both MIT, `@noble/curves` depending on `@noble/hashes` alone, published 2026-08-27. They give secp256k1 recovery and keccak-256; everything else (the EIP-4361 message, EIP-191 hashing, EIP-55, the `ownerOf` and EIP-1271 calls) is a few lines in `server/claimsCrypto.ts` and `server/claimsChain.ts` that can be read in one sitting. The Solana signature, when it comes, uses Node's own ed25519.
2. **What C1 covers.** Options: the two EVM registries first; or EVM and Solana together. **Chosen: Robinhood Chain and BNB Smart Chain first.** The Solana owner read needs the Metaplex Core and 8004 account layouts decoded for a live ownership check, which has not been verified, so it is the next stage and not part of this one. Fleet-level claims are also not in C1: a wallet with 50 or more listings cannot claim a single agent and is told so.

## Where this differs from the plan, and why

- **The server builds the message and compares what it is sent.** The plan has the server parse and validate the message. Rebuilding it from the stored nonce row and comparing byte for byte leaves no parser to get wrong, and makes "a message for another domain, chain, agent or edit" impossible to pass off.
- **Every edit is signed.** The plan's audit log shows a signature digest on each edit, so each edit needs its own signature. The message shows exactly that edit, as JSON, so a wallet shows what is being approved (the independent review found that a hash alone would let a phishing page get an attacker's links signed).
- **Three tables, not two.** The plan names `agent_claims` and `agent_claim_edits`; it also says nonces are stored hashed, which needs `agent_claim_nonces`. A fourth column, `seq` (`AUTO_INCREMENT`), keeps the history in the order it was written, because a timestamp is only to the second. The nonce times are unix seconds, so no session time zone can change a message's text.
- **The per-agent edit limit is counted from the nonces that made an edit,** so it needs no extra table and is read before any chain is asked, and a refused attempt does not count.
- **A failed verification spends its nonce,** and a visitor's chain reads are held to 60 a minute for each chain, so a flood of attempts cannot use the compute units the worker shares.
- **The fleet rule counts the registering wallet too,** and a wallet holds at most 50 claims, so moving agents out of a fleet to fresh wallets does not get around it.
- **A claim is ended only on two agreeing answers** from the registry, and an empty answer is an outage, so a blip cannot discard what an owner wrote.
- **A claim that is on hold blocks other claims for the entry,** not only its owner's edits.
- **No page in C1.** The Claim button, the wallet connection and the edit form are C2 (below). The public history and the owner-provided fields on an agent page are C3. C1 stores what C3 will show.

## What the operator is asked to approve

- The two dependencies above, with the lock file change (20 lines).
- The migration 0042 file, which he runs by hand when he chooses (`docs/claims-2026-10-18.md` has the order, the record to insert, the check and the rollback).
- The limits: 20 requests from one client in ten minutes, 5 messages for one wallet from one client in ten minutes, 20 edits for one agent in a day, 60 chain reads a minute for each chain for visitors' requests, and a daily check of at most 25 claims a run, one or two chain reads each.
- The first-claim rules: the wallet must be the owner the registry records now, must not be a fleet (50 or more listings), and a counterfactual contract wallet is refused.

## What this says and does not say

- **A claim is self-described.** It proves control of the wallet the registry records as the owner, at the time of the claim and of each edit. It does not prove who the owner is, that the agent works, or that what they write is true. The page, when it exists, says so.
- **It is not Tier 3 or Tier 4.** A claimed website can later start a domain proof through the existing flow; that is separate and is not built here.
- **A transfer ends the claim.** The registry is asked at every edit and every day, and the new owner may claim fresh.
- **BNB Chain entries stay acknowledged.** A claim on one adds words the owner wrote beside the entry's chain facts; it does not review, promote or reclassify it.
- **Free text from a registry anyone can register in** is the risk this flow opens: any owner of a BNB or Robinhood registration could write text and links beside it. The limits are the four fields and their sizes, https links only on public names, the owner's signature on each edit, the public append-only history, the operator's hold, and the fact that nothing reads a claim until C3 shows it with its label. Whether and how the owner-written text appears on a page is decided at C3.

## Reviewed independently

The finished code was given to a separate read-only security review, with the files and a threat model and without my conclusions. It found no way to claim or edit an agent without owning it, and four abuse levers (a lockout of a named wallet, free chain spend, a fleet-rule bypass, an edit message that showed only a hash) and several smaller gaps. All were verified and fixed before the commit; the table is in `docs/claims-2026-10-18.md`, with the three things left to check or decide (the proxy count, how owner text is shown, a policy call on buyers of fleet-minted agents).

## Held by tests

The signature maths against the standards' own vectors (the address of the key 1, the EIP-55 examples), a canonical signature only (high-s and every other form refused); the message, byte for byte; the endpoint against a real database with real wallet signatures and a fake chain (30 cases: every refusal, the owner asked at the claim and on each edit, a contract wallet, a change of owner, the fleet rule in all its parts, the limits and who can spend them, the hold, the daily check and its double reading, an empty answer, a hold landing mid-request, the sweep, two claims at once, what is logged, the tables missing); the four fields; the append-only history and the fact that only `server/claims.ts` writes the tables and only the router and the worker import it; fixed messages with no address or key; and no log line with an address. 25 mutations were applied to the checks (the owner check, the message comparison, the nonce use, the edit's re-read of the owner, the edit text, the three parts of the fleet rule, the hold, the counterfactual refusal, the wallet limit's key, spending a failed nonce, counting refused edits, the second reading, the sweep, an empty answer, the revert wording, the read budget, high-s, the recovery value, https, the dotless and the trailing-dot host, and idempotence): 24 are caught. The one that survives, the conditional write for an edit, is a second layer behind a status check in the same transaction.

## Stage C2: the page

**Decided:** 2026-10-18, by the operator, in chat: "start C2". The plan's words for it: an injected EVM wallet, a message preview in plain words ("This only proves you own the wallet. No transaction, no gas."), and an edit form limited to the allowed fields.
**Changes:** a "Claim this agent" section on the page of a Robinhood Chain or BNB Smart Chain entry (a connect button, the message shown in full before it is signed, and the edit form for the holder), one new public read (`claim.state`), and a `claims` entry in `shared/features.ts`. No other page changes.
**Does not change:** anything C1 does not, and no legal page. The wording it needs on the Privacy and Terms pages is not written here.

What the operator did not decide and I chose, so he can say no:

1. **It ships off.** The panel renders only when a build names `claims` in `VITE_FEATURES`, the switch the Watch and Patrols scaffolds already use. The Privacy page says "There are no accounts and no sign-in", which this flow contradicts, and the site's rule is that a page's claims match the code. A merge therefore changes nothing a visitor sees; switching it on is a separate step after the Privacy and Terms wording is cleared.
2. **EVM wallets only.** The server reads EVM registries only, so a Solana wallet would have nothing to prove.
3. **`claim.state` is a new public read.** The page needs to know whether an entry can be claimed and who holds the claim. It asks no chain, writes nothing and never returns a signature. It does return what an owner wrote, to anyone who calls the API; the page does not show that text to other visitors until C3.
4. **The message is shown before the wallet is asked.** The plan asks for a preview in plain words; the page shows the whole EIP-4361 message, which for an edit includes the change as JSON, and offers the signature only after it.
5. **Nothing is kept in the browser,** not even the connected account. A reload shows the claim and asks the visitor to connect again. A test holds the claim client code to this, and to asking the wallet for an account and one signature and nothing else.
6. **No owner text is shown to other visitors in C2.** They see who holds the claim and when the registry was last asked. Where and how an owner's words appear (and whether a link in them is clickable) is the C3 decision.

What the operator is asked to approve for C2: the diff; the page staying off until the legal wording is cleared; the words the panel uses (in `client/src/lib/claimFlow.ts`); and the wording packet for his legal assistant (`legal-review-claims-2026-10-18.md`). The record, the browser check and the switch are in `docs/claims-2026-10-18.md`.

## Stage C3: what the owner wrote and the history

**Decided:** 2026-10-18, by the operator, in chat: "ok proceed", after accepting the legal review's history model. The plan's words: "public edit history on the agent page: front end plus one read endpoint (server, no migration). Shows the claim state and the append-only log."
**Changes:** the owner's text and a claim history inside the "Claim this agent" panel (so behind the same `claims` switch); `claim.history` (public); a hold that hides the text in `claim.state` and `claim.history`; `claim.removeText` and `claim.operatorClaims` (operator only); a "Claims" section on the operator page; the history, hold and removal sentences on the Privacy and Terms pages. No migration.
**Does not change:** a tier, listing, order, a count, the reviewed records, the history table (append-only: a removal is a new row), anything while the switch is off.

What the operator did not decide and I chose, so he can say no:

1. **More than the plan's one endpoint.** The plan's C3 is one read. The legal review accepted by the operator makes removal and a hold that hides text real, and the pages promise both, so there are two operator procedures and an operator screen too. Without the screen, holding a claim or removing text would mean a hand-made request to the API.
2. **The history shows the signature digest** (the start of it, beside the signing wallet), as the plan says. It is a hash of a signature that is not public; it ties a history row to a signed message without revealing it.
3. **The history is closed until opened,** and shows the 100 newest rows, saying so when there are more.
4. **A hold hides edits' text in the history too,** not only the current text, so a held claim shows nothing the owner wrote anywhere.
5. **An ended claim's history stays visible** and says why it ended; the old owner's text is not shown in the claim section, because only a claim in force is.
6. **The operator's tool is not behind the switch.** It is on the operator page only, needs the operator's session, and is useful before the panel is switched on, since the server side of claiming is already live and can be called directly.

7. **A removal says who asked.** After the second legal review (the assistant's answer to Q2): the removal row carries "at the owner's request" or "by Nooscape", chosen by the operator, never a reason. The operator tool will not remove text until one is chosen.
8. **Nothing ends a claim except a change of owner.** The Terms say Nooscape may end a claim, but the only operator tools are a hold and a removal. A hold stops edits and hides the text, and blocks another claim; it is the stronger tool in practice. Building an operator "end this claim" is a separate decision.

What the operator is asked to approve for C3: the diff; the operator's Claims section; the words on the panel (`client/src/lib/claimFlow.ts`); and the Privacy and Terms sentences, which go to his legal assistant (the v3 note) before anything merges. The record and the browser check are in `docs/claims-2026-10-18.md`.
