# A page for each chain (Chain Worlds), stage A

Front end only, from reads that already exist. No migration, no new table, no worker step, no change to the listing rules or the gate.

**Status.** Stage A shipped (PR 170, 2026-10-20, main `79d621a`). Stage B1 shipped (PR 171, 2026-10-20, main `b485665`). Stage B2 shipped (PR 172, 2026-10-20, main `a516e18`). Stage B3 shipped (PR 173, 2026-10-20, main `954d733`). Stage C, below, the Arc page, shipped with the Arc arc (PR 178, 2026-10-20, main `ed8ade1`). Stage D, the Base page, is built and tested on the branch `feature/base-world`, in a pull request with auto-merge off ([the record](./base-world-2026-10-21.md)).

**Decided:** 2026-10-20, by the operator, in chat: "i am open to making dedicated pages to each chain so we can really make them shine", then "BNB groups-only, start Stage A  if the existing icons are to big or hard then make new ones and be creative", and asked that no generated tooling files or planning filler come with it.

**Where it came from.** An outside draft (a plan, a to-do and a source archive of the site as of 19 Oct 2026) proposed one interactive page for each chain with small robot characters and a replay of recorded events. It was built on the then-current `main` and looked at in a browser with live reads (the review is in `todo.md` item 113). Its idea and its honesty rules were kept. It had layout faults, a conflict with the BNB rule, and noise, and this stage fixes those.

## What a visitor sees

Three pages, `/chains/robinhood`, `/chains/solana` and `/chains/bnb`, each with the chain's own colour and its own backdrop, a row of figures for the chain, a card for whatever is selected, and a list of recorded events beside it. The home page's chain section links to all three.

- **Formations.** A group of registrations that share one fact is drawn as a stack of plates in the chain's outline: a disc for BNB Chain, a diamond for Solana, a hexagon for Robinhood Chain, the same outlines as the lights on the home map. Its count is written under it. Its size grows with the log of the count, between 56 and 100 pixels, and says nothing else. A formation is never an agent. Robinhood Chain shows its five registering wallets (they hold 88 percent of its listed entries on 19 Oct 2026), Solana its six owner wallets, BNB Chain its ten largest groups.
- **Scouts.** A reviewed record or an entry the directory lists is a small scout. The same id always draws the same one (36 in all: three bodies, three pairs of eyes, four crowns). A reviewed record is drawn solid and an entry that is not reviewed is drawn hollow and dashed, so the difference is in the outline and not in colour alone. The scouts are illustrations and the page says so.
- **BNB Chain is groups only.** As on the home map (docs/bsc-fleet-lights-2026-10-16.md and invariant 23), no single BNB Chain registration is drawn. A reviewed BNB Chain record would be, beside the groups, and there is none today. Search on that page matches the groups and reviewed records only, and the registry filter is hidden where there is one registry.
- **The card for a group** uses the same sentences as the home map's lights (`fleetFact`, `fleetNotProved`, `fleetOverlapNote`), so the two cannot drift, and links the dated census where there is one (BNB Chain, Solana).
- **The recorded events.** Robinhood Chain shows the identity events the activity walk measured (through a stated block), and Solana the anchored registration, owner and profile events, with the pass's slot named as an observation slot and Nooscape's observed time kept apart from it. BNB Chain says there is no global feed and shows no event in its place. A replay is explicit, goes one recorded event at a time, stops at the newest and never starts under reduced motion. While it plays, the event is named in a banner at the foot of the stage, with its block or slot and, for a transfer, "RECORDED OWNERSHIP CHANGE / NOT A MESSAGE" and the two recorded addresses.

## What changed from the draft

1. **The event list is not cut off.** The rail's two columns needed 530 pixels and its column is 335. It now lays out by its own width (a container query), one column in the sidebar and two where there is room.
2. **Nothing stands on anything.** The draft put 16 large figures on a jittered grid, so labels ran into the next row. `client/src/lib/chainWorldLayout.ts` gives every figure and formation a cell bigger than its drawing and its one-line label, and makes the stage as tall as its cells need. The figures that do not fit are left out (16 on a wide stage, 9 on a narrow one). Only the one under the pointer or focus, and the selected one, show a second line.
3. **Formations first.** The draft cut the scene to the first 16 entries, with the groups last, so BNB Chain showed 16 individual registrations and none of its groups. Groups are now laid out first and the figures after them.
4. **New, smaller figures.** The draft's characters were about 90 pixels tall with up to three lines of text each, and each carried eight gradient and filter definitions of its own and about nine animations. The scouts are 56 by 64 with no gradients, and each moves with one animation: 21 for 21 items on the Robinhood Chain page, against 147 in the draft. Reduced motion runs none.
5. **Robinhood Chain draws its groups** from the new `mapFleets.robinhood` field, and a group is put on its own chain's registry (the draft would have called a Robinhood Chain group a Solana one). The card says what is true of a wallet group (a registration is counted in at most one light) and not that groups "may overlap".
6. **Smaller diffs.** The draft's reformatting of `routeHead.ts`, `routeHead.test.ts` and `clientRoutes.ts` (about 300 lines) is not here: only the three routes and the three heads are. Its client-set page title is not here either, because the server writes each page's own head. Its root planning files and a generated routes file are not part of this.
7. **Times** are worded in UTC (9 Oct 2026, 03:27 UTC), not as raw ISO text.

## Stage B1: a composition for each world

**Decided:** 2026-10-20, by the operator, in chat: "start stage B". Stage B is split to keep the stops between stages: this part is the composition, the Robinhood Chain concentration fact, a wider spread of formation sizes and the links from the home map. The dated Robinhood Chain census report and the share cards wait for their own stops.

- **Zones.** Each world is made of plates, and a thing stands on the plate whose name says what it is. Solana has a lane for each registry (Metaplex, 8004, SAID), and a group or a record stands in the lane of the registry it is recorded in. The SAID lane says why it has no groups, in the home map's own reason. BNB Chain has a platform for each kind of shared fact (same host, same profile link, same registering wallet, same registration template), and each group stands on the platform of the fact it counts. Robinhood Chain has a plate for the registering wallets and a wider plate for the identities. A plate with nothing on it is not drawn. The stage is as tall as its plates need and a narrow stage stacks them. `client/src/lib/chainWorldLayout.ts` sets them out and `worldZones` in the model says what stands on each.
- **Names wait for the pointer.** A scout is drawn without its name, so 24 of them can stand on a plate without running their labels together. The name and the registry show for the scout under the pointer or focus, and for the selected one, and stay inside the stage at its edges. Formations keep their title and count. Every name is also in the card, the list below the stage and the button's own label, which now names the plate as well.
- **More scouts.** A wide stage shows up to 24 figures and a narrow one 12, shared out round and round between the plates, so every plate with something on it shows some. Robinhood Chain shows all 18 of its reviewed records and the entries from recorded activity after them.
- **A wider spread of sizes.** A formation grows with the log of its count from 48 pixels at 100 to 112 at 130,000. A row of small ones takes less room than a row of large ones, and the drawings in a row hang from one line.
- **The Robinhood Chain page leads with who registered the entries.** A bar of the entries the directory lists, cut by the wallet each is recorded against, and a row of those wallets with their counts and shares: with the directory's counts of 19 Oct 2026, "88 percent of the 8,882 entries the directory lists on Robinhood Chain are recorded against 5 registering wallets" (the page says what the directory holds when it is read, and by 20 Oct a sixth wallet had passed 100). The percent is rounded down so it never claims more than it is. It is one figure from one read, because the wallets and the count are taken together (`mapFleets.robinhood.listed`), and if the count cannot be read, or is smaller than the wallets', no share is said. The sentence under it is the card's own ("a registering wallet is the address that registered these identities ... it does not say who holds them now, who runs the agents, or whether any of them works"), says when the directory was read and that the other entries are recorded against wallets with fewer than 100 each. A wallet in the row selects its formation.
- **The home map's lights link here.** A light's card now ends with "Open the Robinhood Chain page" (or the Solana or BNB Chain page). It is the only link on the card: no light opens an agent, an entry or a record.

**Held by a test.** `chainWorldLayout.test.ts`: at 12 widths from 260 to 1800 pixels, in six worlds (none, one plate, a split, three lanes, four platforms, far too many) and both arrangements, no two things overlap, every thing is inside its own plate below the plate's name, plates do not cross and stay inside the stage and clear of the title bar and the event banner; the plate rows; the split; the figure shares; the sizes. `chainExplorerModel.test.ts`: which plate each group and record stands on for each chain, the SAID lane's reason, an empty plate left out, the share (rounded down, ordered the same way every time, nothing when the reads disagree). `landingHero.test.ts`: the one link on a light's card is to its chain's page and none opens an agent. `mapFleets.test.ts` and `mapFleets.integration.test.ts`: `listed`, against a real database, with hidden and closed entries taken out and nothing when there are no wallets.

**Checked.** On the built server, on a scratch database seeded to the live proportions (8,882 Robinhood Chain entries, 7,819 of them on five wallets), with every other read answered from the live site, in headless Chrome at 1440, 1180, 900 and 375 pixels on all three pages: no overlaps, everything inside its plate and the stage, the event list fits, no horizontal overflow and no console error. Keyboard arrows, Home and End move between all the figures and formations and Escape clears; a focused scout shows its name inside the stage; a wallet in the share row opens that wallet's card; animations are one for each thing (29, 30 and 10) and none under reduced motion; the banner stands clear of the plates; and from the home map each light's link lands on its chain's page.

## Stage B2: the Robinhood Chain census report

**Decided:** 2026-10-20, by the operator, in chat: "start B2". It is built like the Solana census ([the measurement](./solana-census-2026-10-18.md)): a measurement document, a typed data file the page reads, and a test that holds the page to both. Front end and docs only. No migration, no server change, no new read.

- **The measurement.** [`robinhood-census-queries-2026-10-20.sql`](./robinhood-census-queries-2026-10-20.sql) holds eleven read-only queries that return counts and size buckets and no name, wallet address or host name. The operator ran them in one sitting against production on 2026-10-20, between the registry pass that finished at 02:15 UTC and the next one, and [the output is kept as it came back](./robinhood-census-2026-10-20.md).
- **The report.** `/field/robinhood-registry-2026-10-20` says what the directory held for the registry: 9,191 entries on 771 registering wallets. Six wallets are each recorded against 100 or more entries and hold 8,137 of them (88.5%), the largest 5,593. Five of the six have every entry on one host name, and the largest wallet's entries are the whole of the largest host name. 8,064 entries (87.7%) still carry the placeholder name. 6,892 entries (75.0%) were handed out within about an hour of the mint. 642 ratings were written by 8 wallets, and two of them wrote 584. Each is a count over recorded facts. The page names no host, wallet or agent, ranks nothing and gives no score, and it says what the counts do not show (the registering wallet is not the holder, a hand-out is a timing fact, a shared host or wallet is not one person).
- **Shares to one decimal place.** The Robinhood Chain page rounds its headline share down to a whole number ("88 percent") and gives one decimal for each wallet. The report gives one decimal everywhere, rounded to the nearest, so the two can sit side by side. A test runs the chain page's own `registrationShare` on the census's six wallets and checks the headline is the floor of the report's 88.5 and each chip equals the report's figure.
- **Held by a test.** `robinhoodRegistryReport.test.ts`: every number on the page is one of the data file's figures, a derived figure or a size-bucket label, and none is typed into the page's source; each measured row is in the measurement document as the query returned it, and each worked sentence is in it as the page words it; the totals add up (the entries agree across five queries, the wallet buckets add up to 771 and 9,191, the six large wallets are the two largest buckets, the per-entry buckets add up and fit the totals, the activity ledger agrees with the totals kept for each entry, the ratings by agent and by wallet each add up to the 642 written); the largest wallet's entries are the whole of the largest host name; the queries are reads and carry the site's visibility rule; the page names no address, host or person, uses none of the words the censuses bar (rank, score, tier, best, top, leading, operator) and no long dash; and the route, the head, the sitemap and the four links are in place.
- **Where it is linked.** The home map's caption, the Robinhood Chain tab on the home page, the share note on the Robinhood Chain page, and the card of a selected wallet there (`CHAIN_GROUP_REPORT`).
- **A difference it names.** The ratings figure on the home page counts only ratings of the entries the directory shows. It read 641 when checked after the run, against the ledger's 642 (query 8). The difference was not traced, and the report says the two can differ.

## Stage B3: a share card for each chain page

**Decided:** 2026-10-20, by the operator, in chat: "start B3". Until now a link to any page but the home page previewed with the site's one card, so a post about the Robinhood Chain page looked like a post about any other page. Each chain page now has a card of its own. Static files and the head the server writes: no migration, no new read, no change to a page or to the gate.

- **The cards.** Three 1200 by 630 PNGs in `client/public/` (`nooscape-card-chain-robinhood.png`, `nooscape-card-chain-solana.png`, `nooscape-card-chain-bnb.png`), in the site card's style: the dot grid, the spaced eyebrow with the chain's colour, the world's title in the site's two typefaces, one plain line, and the site's footer promise. The picture is the page's own structure, drawn with the page's own marks and scouts: orbit rings with hexagon wallets and scouts for Robinhood Chain; three registry lanes of diamonds and scouts for Solana, with the SAID lane holding scouts only, as on the page; four platforms of discs for BNB Chain, with no scouts because that page draws groups only. Each says "Illustration, not agent activity". Solana's card was replaced on 2026-10-28 by `nooscape-card-chain-solana-four.png`, with a fourth lane for AgenC ([the lane record](./agenc-lane-2026-10-28.md)).
- **No figure on a card.** A platform keeps a card by its address, so a card carries no count, share or date that its page could outgrow. The sizes of the formations follow the page's own scale and are a picture, not a measurement. A dated card for a census report is a different thing, because a report's figures never change, and it is not part of this.
- **How a page gets its card.** `PageMeta` has an optional `image`: an absolute address and the words that describe it. `injectHead` writes it into `og:image`, `og:image:secure_url`, `twitter:image` and the two alt tags, and leaves the shell's own card when a page has none. The shell's type, width and height stay, because every card is a 1200 by 630 PNG (a test holds it). `ROUTE_HEADS` in `server/routeHead.ts` names each chain page's card file and alt text beside its title and description.
- **File names.** A card gets a new name if its picture ever changes, because the platforms that show cards keep one by its address.
- **How they were made.** From the site's own `FormationMark` and `AgentFigure` drawings and its two typefaces (Space Grotesk and DM Mono, from the packages the site already uses), laid out as a page at 1200 by 630, captured in headless Chrome and saved as lossless PNGs of about 350 KB. The builder is a scratch script and is not kept in the repository, as with the site card.
- **Held by a test.** `server/routeHead.test.ts`: the three chain pages carry a card and no other fixed page does; each is a different 1200 by 630 PNG under 600 KB in the public folder and not the site's card; the alt text names the chain, says the scouts are an illustration, carries the site's two promises and holds no figure or long dash; `injectHead` writes the five tags and leaves the type, width, height, every other tag and the policy built from the shell as they were; a page with no card keeps the shell's; an alt text cannot become markup; and each fixed page, served by the router, carries either its own card or the site's.
- **Checked.** On the built server, against a scratch database, read the way a link-preview crawler reads a page (`Twitterbot` and `facebookexternalhit`): each chain page names its own card in `og:image`, `og:image:secure_url` and `twitter:image`, with the same alt text in both alt tags and the shell's type, width and height; each card is served as `image/png` by GET and by HEAD at 1200 by 630; and nine other pages (the home page, the checker, about, the field, the three census reports, developers and the catch) keep the site's card. In headless Chrome each chain page loads with its own title and card in its head, draws as before and has no script error.

## Stage C: the Arc world

**Decided:** 2026-10-20, by the operator, in chat: "do we have a dedicated page like we have for the other 3 for arc  i want to get that all complete before moving on to base". Arc had a tab, search, entry pages and the API but no page of its own: the multichain work kept Ethereum, Base and Arc out of the chain list on purpose ([the record](./multichain-erc8004-2026-10-20.md), "Where the extra chains stop"). This stage gives Arc the first of the four things the other chains have. The map lights, the share card and the Owl's Catch lines wait for their own stops. No migration, no new table, no worker step: a page, a route behind the `chain_arc` switch and two public reads.

- **The page.** `/chains/arc`, World 04, "The glass harbor", in Arc's own light blue (`#ACC6E9`, the primary colour of arc.network, with its violet `#9F72FF` for the lines and stars). It is behind `chain_arc`. The route is registered only when the build has the switch, the server answers 404 for it otherwise (`FLAGGED_CLIENT_ROUTES`), and its head and its place in the sitemap are written only while the switch is on. The page's links to the other worlds and the home tab's "Open the Arc page" follow the same switch. The other three pages are as they were.
- **What it shows.** The three figures every chain has (ratings say "Not measured") and the sentence the home tab has ("The chain holds N registrations. The published listing rules put M of them in the directory; the rest are held out, not judged."). Then the block the Robinhood Chain page leads with, over the registrations the registry holds, listed or not: with the figures of the scratch registry it was checked on, "83 percent of the 2,721 registrations on Arc are recorded against 7 registering wallets", a bar, and a row of the wallets with their counts and shares. The headline is rounded down and the note says what a registering wallet does not show. It counts the registry and not the directory because the listing rules hold out every wallet that registered ten or more, so the directory's entries hold none of the wallets that matter here. Then the stage: a plate for each kind of shared fact, in the order wallet, host, profile link, with a formation for each group (a dome, the outline of Arc's tab icon), and a plate of entries.
- **The groups.** The registering wallets, hosts and profile links that 50 or more registrations share (`FLEET_MIN`, the minimum of the lights on the home map), counted over every registration the registry holds, listed or held out, because the groups are what holds registrations out (`readRegistryGroups` in `server/agentRegistryRows.ts`). A registration that is hidden, by the operator or because it points at a suppressed host, is counted in none. The read is held in the directory cache and answers nothing until a walk that reached the finalized head has published, because before that the stored rows are only a part of the chain. At most eight groups of a kind are drawn and all are counted (`walletGroups` says whether the wallets drawn are all the wallets at the rule). A link is called by its host, or by a name its members record, never by the placeholder every unnamed registration gets; a name is cleaned for public text. A group's id is derived from the shared fact and is not the fact. Each group says what it counts in BNB Chain's words ("868 registrations on Arc point their profile link at ...") and what the shared fact does not show.
- **The entries.** A fixed sample of the entries the directory lists (the 30 whose ids hash lowest, as the home map's BNB Chain sample is picked), drawn hollow and dashed as acknowledged and never reviewed. Any reviewed Arc record (there is none yet) comes first, and the search adds matches. This is what Robinhood Chain and Solana do. BNB Chain's groups-only rule was the operator's decision for BNB Chain; here it is one constant (`CHAIN_SHOWS_ACKNOWLEDGED.arc`) if he wants it the other way.
- **The events.** Arc has no global feed, as BNB Chain has none. The rail says so and shows the events recorded for the selected entry, from that entry's own activity.
- **Not in this stage, and built after it.** Map lights, a share card, a dated census report, "just arrived", the Owl's Catch, the watchlist and the webhook, and claims were left for the stages that followed. Each is a record of its own: [the lights](./arc-lights-2026-10-20.md), [the census](./arc-census-2026-10-20.md) (the listing rules, which are BNB Chain's numbers, stay provisional; the census reports what they did and does not say whether they are right), [the share card](./arc-share-card-2026-10-20.md), ["New to the Owl"](./arc-arrivals-2026-10-20.md), [the Owl's Catch](./arc-owls-catch-2026-10-20.md), [the watchlist and the webhook](./arc-watch-2026-10-20.md) and [claims](./arc-claims-2026-10-20.md), which are behind a switch of their own (`claims_arc`).
- **Held by a test.** `registryFleets.test.ts`: the largest groups of each kind, the limit, the tie break, a name and a link's label, no placeholder as a label, ids that are not the fact, a name cleaned for public text, the count of what the registry holds, and no field of any one registration. `registryFleets.integration.test.ts`, against a real MySQL: the wallets, hosts and links at the minimum counted over listed and held-out registrations alike, a hidden registration counted in none, another chain's rows not counted, nothing before a walk has published or while the source is paused, the public reads answering nothing while the switch is off, and nothing of a registration in the answer. `chainExplorerModel.test.ts`: the worlds on offer for each switch list, what is drawn on Arc and never of another chain, the plates in a fixed order, the share rounded down and whether the wallets are all of them. `mapFleets.test.ts`: Arc's words, its outline, and four outlines for four chains. `clientRoutes.test.ts`, `routeHead.test.ts` and `sitemap.test.ts`: the route, its head and its place in the sitemap only while the switch is on, and no page for Base or Ethereum when theirs are on. `chainTabsModel.test.ts`: the page link.
- **Checked.** On the built server (built with `chain_arc` on, against a scratch database with a registry in the shape the chain showed on 2026-10-20: 2,721 registrations, seven wallets with 50 or more, a link shared by 246, 244 listed), in headless Chrome at 1440 and 375 pixels: the plates, the 12 formations and the scouts (24 on the wide stage, 12 on the narrow one) drawn with no overlap and every figure inside its plate, no horizontal overflow and no console error. A wallet in the share row, a formation and a scout each open their card, the arrow keys, Home, End and Escape move and clear, a scout with recorded events shows them in the rail, and the home tab links to the page. On the same build run with every switch off, `/chains/arc` answers 404, the sitemap does not list it, both reads answer nothing and `chainStats` has no `extras`.

## What is read

Only public reads that exist: `liveData.chainStats` (the figures and the as-of line), `publicRecords` (the reviewed records), `directoryActivityFeed` (Robinhood Chain events), `directorySolanaActivity` (Solana events), `mapFleets` (the groups, all three chains, and for Robinhood Chain how many entries the directory lists), `directorySearch` (Robinhood Chain and Solana only) and `agentProfile` (the selected entry). Stage B1 adds one number to the server, `mapFleets.robinhood.listed`. Stage C adds two reads for Arc's page, `registryFleets` (the groups) and `chainSample` (the sample of listed entries), which answer nothing while `chain_arc` is off. A read that fails says so on the page, and an empty feed is never shown as a zero.

## Checked

On a build of this branch in headless Chrome with every read answered from the live site, for the three pages at 1440, 1180, 900 and 375 pixels: no two things overlap, everything is inside the stage, the event list fits its card, there is no horizontal overflow and no console error.
