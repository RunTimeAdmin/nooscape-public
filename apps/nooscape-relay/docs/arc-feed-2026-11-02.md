# The Arc feed (Chain Worlds, stage G)

One server read and some page wiring, from data that already exists. No migration, no new table, no worker step, no change to the listing rules or the gate.

**Status.** Merged as PR 247 on 2026-11-02 at 03:56Z (branch `feature/arc-activity-feed`, main 3f37f31) and live at 03:56:59 GMT, behind `chain_arc`, which production has on. "Checked" below says what was seen on a built server, and "Live" what was seen after the deploy.

**Decided:** 2026-11-02, by the operator, in chat. He asked "how do we add the global feeds for some of the other chains". The options were laid out: a feed over the entries the directory lists, chain by chain, with Arc the one whose page draws entries. He answered "build Arc". The four pages that draw groups only (BNB Chain, Base, Ethereum, Monad) are left as they are; see "Not in this stage".

## What a visitor sees

- **The rail on `/chains/arc`.** "Historical chain activity" lists the newest 40 recorded events of the entries the directory lists on Arc, newest first: identity registered, registration hand-out, ownership transferred, profile link updated. Each shows its block, its transaction hash and the two recorded addresses, and links its entry. The replay, the previous and next buttons and the timeline work as they do on Robinhood Chain's page, and picking or replaying an event puts its entry on the stage (an acknowledged figure, "Entry from recorded activity, not reviewed") and in the inspector. The coverage line reads "Listed entries · measured through block N", where N is the highest block the Arc ledger holds. Before this the rail said "Global feed unavailable" until an entry was selected, and then showed that entry's own events, the oldest 24 at most.
- **What it does not show.** A registration the listing rules hold out has events in the ledger and is never named here: Arc holds 3,277 registrations and the directory lists 418 (13 percent, from the stats API on 2026-11-02). There is no time: the ledger stores block numbers, so the rail says Block and "Nooscape observed time: Not recorded", as Robinhood Chain's does. It is a rear-view mirror, "Historical replay · not live".
- **Nothing else moves.** The figures, the groups, the share block and the sample of listed entries are as they were. The feed adds a figure only for an entry that has an event among its 40.

## What is read

`liveData.chainActivityFeed({ chain: "arc" })` returns `{ feed, measuredThroughBlock }` from `readChainActivityFeed("erc8004_arc")` in `server/db.ts`.

- The scan is Robinhood Chain's feed scan (`readAgentDirectoryActivityFeedUncached`), which now takes a protocol and keeps `erc8004` as its default, so Robinhood Chain's feed is unchanged (`server/evmActivityScoping.test.ts` holds it). It names the chain's active sources, joins the ledger to the listed entries under the directory's own visibility rule (nothing hidden, closed, on a suppressed host, from an inactive source, or on a chain whose switch is off), and takes the newest 40 by block and log index.
- A hand-out is classified by Arc's own mint hour (`handoutBlockWindow`, 7,080 blocks, about an hour at 0.508 s a block), not Robinhood Chain's 36,000.
- Names go through `sanitizeDirectoryEntry`, as the names in the sample of listed entries do.
- `measuredThroughBlock` is the highest block of any event in Arc's ledger, held-out registrations included. It says how far the walk has recorded, not how far the feed's entries go. It is null with no active source.
- The answer is held in the directory cache (`directory:chain-activity-feed:erc8004_arc:40`), warmed at boot and after writes (`server/warmReads.ts`), and fails closed to an empty feed with the cause in the server log. It answers an empty feed while `chain_arc` is off, without reading the database, and takes no chain but Arc.
- The cost is two small reads over `agent_directory_activity_block_idx` (source and block) and the mint anchors of the 40 rows' entries. No index is added.

## What is held by a test

- `server/chainActivityFeed.test.ts`, on MySQL 8.4 and MariaDB 10.11, with Robinhood Chain and Arc in one ledger: the events of the listed entries only, newest block first and the later log first within a block; no event of a hidden entry or of a registration the directory does not list, though both are in the ledger; a high-water block that counts every event in the ledger; a hand-out by Arc's own mint hour (the last block of the hour, the next block, and 10,000 blocks, which is inside Robinhood Chain's hour); a name cleaned of control and invisible characters; Robinhood Chain's feed and totals unchanged and none of its rows in Arc's; an empty answer and no block while no source is active; the public read only while `chain_arc` is on and for Arc only; 40 events at most, the newest.
- `client/src/lib/chainExplorerModel.test.ts`: Arc is not one of the worlds that show an event only for the selected entry (BNB Chain, Base, Ethereum and Monad still are); an Arc event draws its entry as an acknowledged figure, and an event of another chain draws nothing on Arc's page.
- `client/src/components/chain/chainComponents.test.ts`: Arc's rail shows the entry, the block and the coverage from its feed and says neither "global feed unavailable" nor "select an entry"; an empty feed reads "not a live zero", not as a missing feed.
- `server/warmReads.test.ts` and `server/dbBreakerWiring.test.ts`: the feed is warmed with the other public reads, and a paused database or a refusal is logged once, not once a request.

## Checked

Six planted bugs, each caught by a test: the feed filtered by Robinhood Chain's protocol, Robinhood Chain's hand-out window borrowed, the chain's switch ignored by the public read, names not cleaned, the visibility rule dropped, and Arc left among the entry-event worlds.

On a built server (`VITE_FEATURES=chain_arc`) against a scratch MySQL 8.4 database in the shape of Arc's registry (2,721 registrations; 14 listed entries given mints, hand-outs, later transfers and link edits; one held-out registration with a newer event than any of them), in headless Chrome at 1440 and at 375 pixels: the rail lists 30 events newest first and reads "Listed entries · measured through block 29,999,999" (the held-out registration's block); the replay starts at the oldest event; a picked event shows its facts and its entry link, and its entry appears in the inspector as "Acknowledged · not reviewed"; there is no horizontal overflow and no console error. BNB Chain's page is unchanged in code. The scratch database has no BNB source, so its unchanged rail text was read on the live site.

## Live

They are 20 registrations and 20 profile-link updates between blocks 28,833,458 and 28,837,706: about 36 minutes of Arc blocks, and about 18 hours before the ledger's high-water, because Arc is quiet (418 listed of 3,277 registrations, none new in 24 hours). ## Not in this stage

- **BNB Chain, Base, Ethereum and Monad.** Their pages draw groups only and say "Individual registrations are not drawn here", and a feed of listed entries names individual registrations. The slice the directory lists is thin: BNB Chain 6,961 of 401,364 registrations (1.7 percent), Base 9,144 of 1,150,014 (0.8), Ethereum 5,206 of 56,084 (9) and Monad 144 of 10,421 (1.4), from the stats API on 2026-11-02. An unfiltered Base feed would be one platform's mints ([the flood cap](./flood-host-cap-2026-10-21.md)), and counts from the ledger would undercount Base, because the cap drops the events of tokens it only counts. Giving one of these pages a feed is the operator's call, and it changes what the page says it draws. The code to change is the chain list and `ChainFeedProtocol` of the read, `ENTRY_EVENT_WORLDS` and the page's branches, and the rail's empty-state words.
- **A time for each event.** The ledger holds block numbers only. A block-to-time map would need a block read for each event shown.
